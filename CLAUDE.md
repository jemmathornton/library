# Jemma’s Library

A private website of the books in Jemma’s home library: every book she owns, shelf by shelf, what she’s reading and has read, and a to-buy list. Started 18 Oct 2026 as a prototype in the style of her Sims site (`C:\Users\Jemma\Documents\SimTracker`): a static site on her PC, built so it can go online later. Open things are in `TODO.md`.

## Working rules (as for her Sims site)

- **Never edit Jemma’s own files without asking** (Downloads, Documents, Pictures…). Copy them into the project and work on the copy.
- **British English** in everything shown; **curly apostrophes** in displayed text (ids, links and data keys stay straight).
- **Short, plain answers.** She reviews by looking at the site.
- **Obvious bugs** can be fixed straight away; **anything needing her judgement** (shelves, titles, names, layout) goes on `TODO.md`.
- **No AI-generated art.** Covers are the publishers’ own (Open Library / Google Books); a book with no cover gets a plain title card. Icons are Lucide (ISC).
- **Her colours: yellows and browns.** No green (she hates the green, 18 Oct). Banners are made of the page’s own covers (her choice, 18 Oct; since L1, 21 Nov, a plate of them on a paper title page rather than fading into walnut); shelf colours appear only as dots (and, as in the L1 mock-up she chose, tint the cloth of the home page’s shelf cards).
- **Sashes across the top corner** (cream): Read, Reading, DNF (didn’t finish), Paused (her choices, 18 Oct).
- **Series covers match** (her rule, 18 Oct): every book in a series shows the same edition's design, whatever copy she owns; where several complete designs exist she chooses from pictures of each set (the choice boxes can't show images, so send a picture first). ## How she likes it done (moved here from the Sims project’s notes, 19 Nov: the library keeps its own)

- **Covers, same printing:** a whole series from one printing (same publisher edition and year) matching her copies; lean to UK printings (she’s in the UK; Open Library pictures are often US). Flat, uncropped scans. Show each series as one row in reading order (her copies plus the proposal) and **ask before every cover change**, even her own ISBN’s picture. Libib ISBNs can be wrong: believe her description over the ISBN, and offer her own photos as the sure fix. UK sources: Oxfam’s shop API `https://onlineshop.oxfam.org.uk/ccstore/v1/products/HD_<n>` (ox_isbn13, ox_year, primaryFullImageURL + `&height=940`; one shop’s listings sit on neighbouring numbers) and World of Books `https://image-server.worldofbooks.com/images/<isbn13>.jpg`.
- **No generated text:** text on the site comes only from her words or the book’s own blurb, word for word; cuts only, never rewording. Missing means blank.
- **General fixes, not per item:** a problem on one book means fixing the rule for every book; say so in one line when reporting.
- **Show, don’t ask, for looks:** mock each option in the app, screenshot at 1280px, send them labelled, then ask. Words are fine for facts.
- **Suggestions are labelled** “my suggestion (not decided)”, and say who decided what.
- **TODO.md holds only open items;** a decided non-issue comes off.
- **Restarting the server:** only `explorer.exe "$env:APPDATA\Microsoft\Windows\Start Menu\Programs\Startup\Jemma's Library server.lnk"`, then check its parent is explorer.exe (started from Claude’s shell it dies with the session, 31 Oct).
- **When she’s away for hours,** keep working through a long backlog in one turn (checks, research, small fixes needing no decision); a final reply stops everything. Never write as if time has passed.
- **Never change Windows security or firewall settings:** she does those herself. Backups are git/GitHub only.

## Where things are

- `site/`: the app (`index.html`, `site.css`, `js/app.js`, `js/icons.js`, `js/data.js` **generated, never edit**). The only folder to upload if it goes online.
- `data/`: the source of truth. `shelves.json` (order, names, colours, which Libib collection), `books.json` (one entry per book; `id` is the Libib id; `displayTitle` overrides the shown title), `to-buy.json` (`maybeOwned` = book ids that look like it), `covers.json` (ISBN, or `id-<id>` for a book found by title, → cover link). `added.json`: books she added on the site (Add a book page; kept apart because a Libib re-import overwrites `books.json`). `bought.json`: to-buy books she has added to a shelf (“Got it”) or already owns; the build leaves them off the to-buy list. `no-cover.json`: Old Book Collection books that stay title cards rather than show a modern reprint’s cover (her rule, 18 Oct: old books never get modern covers). `removed.json`: Libib books she says aren’t real copies or is getting rid of; the build leaves them out. `shelf-moves.json`: shelves she chose for books Libib has on no genre shelf (18 Oct; the build applies them). Books she owns that were never in Libib are in `added.json` (with `why`), linked to her reading in `storygraph-links.json`. `details.json`: description, pages and publisher from Open Library (`tools/fill_details.py`), used only where Libib had none. `name-fixes.json`: Libib author names tidied (applied by the build). `series.json`: series in reading order (only ones whose order is sure; titles can have other spellings after `|`). Shown in place: on the shelves an author’s series sits together in reading order, and a book page says “Book n of …”; no series page (her choice, 18 Oct).
- `imports/libib-<date>/`: her Libib CSV exports as copied in (one per collection). `imports/storygraph-<date>/`: her StoryGraph export.
- `data/storygraph.json`: her reading, from StoryGraph (where she tracks it): status, dates, stars, moods, reviews. `books` = the shelf books (every copy) it matches; entries without `books` are books she doesn’t own (library books, ebooks). Read, reading or paused ones **sit on a genre shelf, cover faded with a walnut “Not owned” band** (her choice, 18 Oct, replacing the old Read elsewhere page): `data/elsewhere-shelves.json` maps StoryGraph title → shelf id (my guesses, for her to check; a new one needs a line there). They aren’t counted in a shelf’s own book count; Owned / Not owned filters on every shelf. Books she only means to read stay off the shelves.
- `data/reading-fixes.json`: reading statuses she has corrected (title → status), applied by the build over the import (Hooked is DNF, not paused; 18 Oct).
- **Phone scanning (30 Oct, her yes):** Binary Eye on her Android phone forwards each scan to `tools/serve.py`'s second listener, port 8021 on the home Wi-Fi (`/scan?key=…&isbn=…` only: a valid 978/979 ISBN with the key goes on `data/scanned.json`, not in git; it serves no pages and can't add books). The key lives outside the project in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Jemmas Library\scan-key.txt`; the address and key show only on the Add a book page's “Set up phone scanning” (laptop only, `/api/scan-setup`). The Add page checks `/api/scanned` every 4 seconds, fills itself in from a new scan, and a book leaves the list when added. Port 8020 stays this-computer-only.
- **New reading comes in by itself:** `tools/serve.py` checks her Downloads every 5 minutes for a newer StoryGraph export (a CSV starting with StoryGraph’s own column list), imports it and rebuilds (only reads Downloads). Last one in `data/storygraph-source.json`, log in `logs/reading-updates.log`.
- `tools/import_storygraph.py <export.csv>` (report; `--matches` lists every match; `--apply` copies the export into imports/ and writes `data/storygraph.json`).
- `tools/import_libib.py <folder>` (report; `--apply` writes `data/books.json` and `data/to-buy.json`: genre collections are the library, the old “Jemma’s Library” catch-all only adds books that aren’t on a shelf, “Books to Buy” is the wishlist). Re-importing overwrites `books.json`, so hand edits there would be lost.
- `tools/find_covers.py` (looks up covers not tried before; `--retry` tries the misses again), `tools/copy_covers.py` (copies them into `site/covers/<isbn>.jpg`, at most 600px tall; the build uses a copy over a link), then `tools/build.py` (writes `site/js/data.js` and stamps fingerprints into index.html).
- App icon: `tools/make_icons.py` (Lucide’s library in yellow on brown; Windows icon `site/app-v2.ico` with every size inside; a new picture needs a new file name, as Windows keeps old icons by name). `tools/serve.py` also answers the Add a book page (`/api/lookup` from Open Library, `/api/books` saves to `added.json` and rebuilds; both need the header `X-Library: 1`; restart it after changing it). Shortcuts: `tools/make_shortcuts.ps1` (Desktop, Start menu, project folder) open http://localhost:8020 as an app window in its own Chrome profile, like the Sims site; `tools/serve.py` serves `site/` there (this computer only), started at login from her Startup folder. `site/manifest.json` and `site/sw.js` let Chrome install it as an app (the only way to a sharp taskbar icon); she prefers the shortcut (18 Oct).

## How to work here

- Python 3.13: `C:/Users/Jemma/AppData/Local/Programs/Python/Python313/python.exe`, run with `-X utf8`. Ask before installing anything.
- **Tests (9 Nov, like her budget app's): `python -X utf8 tests/run_tests.py`** (300 tests, about 90 seconds; `unit` or `e2e` for one half, `-k word` to pick tests; most tests use a made-up 25-book library, `tests/fixture_library.py`). Run them after every change and before every commit; add a test for each bug fixed. `tests/unit/`: the server (adding books, marks, web safety, the reading watcher), the build, both importers, cover straightening, the data check. `tests/e2e/`: the site in headless Chrome (every page and link, pictures, sidebar counts, search, old books, the Add page with a fake server). Every test works on a throwaway copy of the library (`tests/support.py`; the scripts read `LIBRARY_ROOT`), never her real files. `test_build` also checks the site is up to date with data/ (rebuild if it fails there). Quick checks without the suite: `tools/check_data.py`, and `tools/check_pages.js` pasted into the open site.
- Check with the `library` preview (`.claude/launch.json`, port 8011) at 1280px; her app reads the same files through port 8020, so a rebuild shows there at once. Opening `site/index.html` straight from the folder still works too.
- Git repo: commit after each piece of work (`data.js` included). Backed up to GitHub, https://github.com/jemmathornton/library (**public**, her choice 19 Nov: anything committed can be read by anyone); push with `git -c "credential.helper=!'C:/Program Files/GitHub CLI/gh.exe' auth git-credential" push`.
- **Weekly backup:** the Windows task "Jemma’s Library - weekly backup" (Sundays 09:30, `tools/make_backup_task.ps1`) runs `tools/backup.py`: commits changes to the library's own files (LIBRARY_PARTS; anything else new is left out, 21 Nov) and pushes them to GitHub; it writes `site/backup-status.json` (not in git) for the note on Home when a backup failed (no zips since 19 Nov, her call: GitHub keeps every version, off this PC). Log in `logs/backup.log`.
