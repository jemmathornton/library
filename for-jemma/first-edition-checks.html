First-edition checks

29 old books that might be first editions. What to look for in each copy. Tick as you go, or print it.

A Madcap

Your note: Hers is undated, which fits the Cassell edition (Cassell did not date it). Check the publisher on the title page and whether there is a colour frontispiece, but no source can confirm a first from the book alone.

A Tale of Two Cities

Your note: Her note 'Undated, after 1870' rules out the first (1859, dated). Note the publisher and illustrator on the title page to date it; page 213 numbered correctly confirms a later printing.

A World of Girls

Your note: Hers is undated, while the 1886 first and the early reprints carry a date, so it is most likely a later Cassell printing. Look at who drew the pictures: Gertrude A. Steel colour plates would confirm a later one.

Andersen's Fairy Tales

Your note: Hers is undated, so it cannot be a first. Note the publisher, translator and illustrator on the title page; those are what would date it.

Brer Rabbit's a Rascal

Your note: Her 'Undated' doesn't count against it, because Dean books aren't dated reliably. Look at the back of the dust wrapper first and count the numbered titles: 12 means first edition.

Black Beauty

Your note: Hers is undated, and the first is dated 1877 on the title page, so it is a later printing. The publisher's name and address, and any adverts, are what would date it.

Captain of Two Schools

Your note: Her 1950 matches the only dated edition found. If the imprint page says 1950 with no reprint line, it is very probably the first, but no source gives anything more to check.

China Fights Back

Your note: Her note 'Left Book Club Edition' and her year 1938 fit the first British edition. Check the cover wording, the 1938 date with no reprint line, and the Horrabin map.

Claudine at St Clare's

Your note: Her year 1944 matches the first. Check the imprint page (back of the title page) for any date after 1944, then look at the cloth and the colour of the lettering.

Corridors of Power

Your note: Her 1964 matches the Macmillan first, and she has a question mark. Check the back of the title page for any reprint line, the red cloth, and, if there's a wrapper, the Sidney Nolan design with 25s net.

Fifth Formers of St Clare's

Your note: Her 1945 matches the first. The imprint page is the deciding check: '1945' alone is the first; a 1947 or 1949 line means a later impression in the same yellow cloth.

Five Go to Mystery Moor

Your note: Her copy says 1954 and she notes 'First edition', so look first at the back of the title page for any 'second impression' or 'reprinted' line, then at the wrapper flap for an unclipped 7/6 net price.

Five Go to Smuggler's Top

Your note: Her copy says 1945 and she notes 'First edition', which fits. Check the back of the title page for reprint lines, then look for blue cloth, the rope-ladder endpaper, and 6/- net on the wrapper.

Five Have a Mystery to Solve

Your note: Her copy says 1962 and she notes 'First edition'. Look for red cloth with black lettering, picture endpapers, and no 'impression' line on the back of the title page.

Five on a Secret Trail

Your note: Her copy says 1956 and she notes 'First edition'. Check the back of the title page for any 'second' or 'third impression' line, then look for red cloth and 7/6 net on the flap.

Go Ahead, Secret Seven

Your note: Her copy has no year, yet later impressions seem to print 'First published 1953' plus an impression line. Look carefully at the back of the title page and the last page for any date before assuming it is a first.

In the Fifth at Malory Towers

Your note: Her 1950 matches. Check the imprint page for any date after 1950. If the wrapper survives, a Stanley Lloyd wraparound (not a Lilian Buchanan cover) fits the first.

Philips' Universal Atlas

Your note: Her note says 'After 1934', presumably from something in the maps. An edition statement on the title page or its back would date it; there is nothing known that marks a first.

Practical Railway Working

Your note: Her 1915 matches the only edition found. Check that the publisher is Boswell, London, and that there's no 'second edition' line.

Sunday Times Travel and Holiday Guide

Your note: Her copy is 1951, which is the earliest edition found under this title. Check the editors (Russell and Nicholas) and the 591 pages to confirm it is the same edition.

The Island of Adventure

Your note: She has '1944' with a question mark. If the back of the title page shows only 1944 and no impression or reprint line, it is likely a first. Also make sure it is not the 1994 facsimile.

The Model Railway Hobby

Your note: Her copy says 1943, earlier than the 1948 and 1949 dates in catalogues. If 1943 is really printed in it, it may be an early edition, but no source confirms the first year; note exactly what the title page and its back say.

The Mountain of Adventure

Your note: Her note 'First edition reprint' suggests the imprint page already shows a reprint line. If so, it is a first edition but not a first impression. Read exactly what the back of the title page says.

The Mystery of the Invisible Thief

Your note: Her 1950 matches. Look for 'First published in 1950' standing alone on the imprint page, and for a Jean Main wraparound if the wrapper survives.

The New School at Scawdale

Your note: Her 1940 matches the dealer's date. Count the plates (frontispiece plus 3), check for yellow cloth, then look at any list of other Angela Brazil titles for anything later than 1940.

The Queer Adventure

Your note: Her 'Undated, before 1964' fits both editions. The illustrator's name settles it: Norman Meredith means the 1952 Staples first of this title; Joyce A. Johnson means the 1961 World Distributors edition.

The Secret of the Border Castle

Your note: Undated is what a first looks like, so that is no warning sign. Check for red cloth with a red top edge and four pictures (frontispiece plus 3) by Charles Willis.

Secret Seven Win Through

Your note: No year is in her copy, so first look for any impression line on the back of the title page, and check whether the wrapper shows a 50p or other decimal price (a 1970s issue).

Well Done, Secret Seven

Your note: Her copy says 1951 and she notes 'First edition'. Green and cream boards with the Seven holding hands, and no impression line, would fit a first.
